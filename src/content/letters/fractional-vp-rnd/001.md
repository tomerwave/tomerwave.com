There is a number nobody on your team tracks, and it decides how the next six months go. It is the count of problems somebody noticed, decided was not worth the conversation, and worked around instead. It appears in no metric you have. It appears eighteen months later, in the sentence "everybody knew that", usually said by somebody who is leaving.

The mechanism is dull. An engineer raises something. The answer is not no, it is later, or a meeting, or a decision that takes three weeks. By the fourth time they have priced in what raising something costs, and they route around it instead. Nothing was ever refused. They just stopped asking.

> Ask your three most senior engineers, one to one: what did you stop bringing to people?

Half deflect. The other half have been waiting for somebody to ask.
