"Find work to automate" is only half a filter. The other half is deliberate noes.

Unwatched processes, disagreements dressed as workflows, quietly expensive failure modes, monthly-changing ops, low volume that cannot pay for care, accountability steps that need a human name, and reports with no reader: those belong on a kill-or-wait list, not in an agent sprint.

This month's essay gives a six-question afternoon table so every candidate gets a verdict: automate, delete, wait, or never unsupervised. Saying no is how you protect the few automations that should still be trusted in a year.

> Tip: if exceptions are already the majority, you are not looking at an AI problem. You are looking at a process that is not ready to be encoded.

If the backlog is politicised and the room cannot kill sacred cows, an opportunity audit includes the explicit do-not-automate set on purpose.
