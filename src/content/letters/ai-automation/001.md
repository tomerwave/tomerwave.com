There's an automation somewhere in your operation that still runs, and nobody trusts. It hasn't broken. It just got quietly wrong often enough that somebody started checking it by hand, and now everyone does.

That's worse than a broken one. A broken automation gets fixed because it stops. This one keeps producing output, so it never raises an alarm, and it's now costing you the manual work it replaced plus the compute to run it plus the checking. The person who built it has usually left, which is why nobody's touched it.

> Ask your ops team which automations they check by hand before they believe them.

The list is never empty, and it's never the one you'd have guessed.
