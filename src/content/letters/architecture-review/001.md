This week ownership stopped being a soft culture word and showed up as an org redesign. Expedia is cutting senior tech leaders and pushing work into small squads with clear ownership so AI can sit inside the work instead of across handoffs. That only works if someone can answer "who owns this surface?" without a meeting.

Most seed to Series A teams hit the same wall earlier, around ten to fifteen engineers, without the press release. Shipping still looks fine. Then billing, auth, or the data path has three owners on paper and zero when something breaks.

The companion essay is a fortnight DIY: list surfaces, put one living name on each, force the map into incidents and design review, then use ten recent tickets to see whether the architecture can even be owned.

> Tip: if most non-trivial changes touch three "owned" surfaces, you do not have an org-chart problem first. You have a boundary problem wearing an ownership costume.
