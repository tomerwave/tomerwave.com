You can feel ownership getting fuzzy before anyone names it.

Work still ships. Incidents still close. Then a simple question ("who owns billing?") gets three answers, or a shrug, and a week of almost appears on the board. At six engineers the map was ambient. Past ten it has to be written, or it quietly stops being true.

This month's companion essay walks through a fortnight DIY: list surfaces (not teams), put one living name on each, force the map into incident routing and design review, then use ten recent tickets to see whether the architecture can even be owned cleanly.

> Tip: if most non-trivial changes touch three "owned" surfaces, you do not have an org-chart problem first. You have a boundary problem wearing an ownership costume.

If you want a structured pass on whether your system can hold real owners, that is the architecture review. The essay stands alone either way.
